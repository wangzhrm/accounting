Impact of Transactions on Profit and Cash Position

This page categorizes common business transactions and events based on their effect on a company's profit (net income) and cash position (cash and cash equivalents). Transactions are grouped into four categories: those affecting cash position only, profit only, both, or neither. Each category includes examples with descriptions and journal entries.

1. Transactions Affecting Cash Position Only

These transactions involve cash inflows or outflows but do not impact profit, as they are typically financing or investing activities or settlements of prior accruals.

Transaction Description Journal Entry
Issuing stock for cash Company receives $50,000 cash by issuing new shares. 
Dr. Cash                  50,000\nCr. Common Stock          50,000
Paying accounts payable Company pays $10,000 in cash to settle accounts payable. 
Dr. Accounts Payable      10,000\nCr. Cash                  10,000
Borrowing from a bank Company borrows $25,000 in cash from a bank. 
Dr. Cash                  25,000\nCr. Notes Payable         25,000
Paying dividends Company pays $4,000 in cash dividends to shareholders. 
Dr. Retained Earnings     4,000\nCr. Cash                  4,000
Receiving cash for prior services Company receives $10,000 cash for services performed last month. 
Dr. Cash                  10,000\nCr. Accounts Receivable   10,000
Receiving advance payment Company receives $5,000 cash for services to be performed next month. 
Dr. Cash                  5,000\nCr. Unearned Revenue      5,000

2. Transactions Affecting Profit Only

These transactions involve revenues, expenses, or gains/losses recognized on an accrual basis or non-cash adjustments, with no immediate cash flow.

Transaction Description Journal Entry
Recording depreciation Company records $5,000 depreciation expense for the month. 
Dr. Depreciation Expense   5,000\nCr. Accumulated Depreciation 5,000
Performing services on credit Company performs $15,000 of services on credit, to be paid next month. 
Dr. Accounts Receivable   15,000\nCr. Service Revenue       15,000
Writing off uncollectible accounts Company writes off $1,000 of uncollectible accounts receivable. 
Dr. Bad Debt Expense      1,000\nCr. Accounts Receivable   1,000
Accruing interest expense Company accrues $3,000 in interest expense, not yet paid. 
Dr. Interest Expense      3,000\nCr. Interest Payable      3,000
Recognizing prepaid expense Company recognizes $2,000 of prepaid insurance used this month. 
Dr. Insurance Expense     2,000\nCr. Prepaid Insurance     2,000
Revaluation gain (IFRS) Company records a $7,000 gain on revaluation of land. 
Dr. Land                  7,000\nCr. Revaluation Surplus   7,000

3. Transactions Affecting Both Cash Position and Profit

These transactions involve cash flows and simultaneously affect revenues, expenses, or gains/losses, impacting profit.

Transaction Description Journal Entry
Paying utility bills Company pays $2,000 in cash for utility bills incurred this month. 
Dr. Utility Expense   2,000\nCr. Cash              2,000
Selling equipment with gain Company sells equipment for $8,000 cash, with a book value of $6,000. 
Dr. Cash                  8,000\nCr. Equipment             6,000\nCr. Gain on Sale          2,000
Cash sale of services Company recognizes $6,000 revenue for services performed, paid in cash. 
Dr. Cash                  6,000\nCr. Service Revenue       6,000
Paying salaries Company pays $10,000 in cash for salaries incurred this month. 
Dr. Salary Expense        10,000\nCr. Cash                  10,000

4. Transactions Affecting Neither Cash Position nor Profit

These transactions affect other balance sheet accounts (assets, liabilities, or equity) but do not involve cash or profit.

Transaction Description Journal Entry
Purchasing inventory on credit Company purchases $20,000 of inventory on credit. 
Dr. Inventory             20,000\nCr. Accounts Payable      20,000
Purchasing a Treasury Bill Company purchases a 30-day Treasury Bill for $10,000 cash. 
Dr. Cash Equivalents      10,000\nCr. Cash                  10,000
Issuing stock for equipment Company issues $15,000 in stock to acquire equipment. 
Dr. Equipment             15,000\nCr. Common Stock          15,000
Recording a note payable for equipment Company acquires $30,000 equipment by signing a note payable. 
Dr. Equipment             30,000\nCr. Notes Payable         30,000
