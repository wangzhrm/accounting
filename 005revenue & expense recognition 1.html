Criteria for Recognition

Revenue or expenses are recognized when:

  • Probability of Economic Benefits: It is probable that economic benefits will flow to or from the entity.
  • Reliable Measurement: The amount of revenue or expense can be measured with reasonable certainty.

If either criterion is not met, recognition is deferred.

1. Bakery Cash Sale

A bakery sells a cake for $50, and the customer pays in cash at the counter. Should the revenue be recognized?

Answer: Yes, recognize $50 revenue.

Explanation:

  • Probability: Payment received in cash, so inflow is certain.
  • Measurement: The amount ($50) is fixed and known.

Both criteria are met, so revenue is recognized immediately.

2. Doubtful Software Project

A software developer completes a $10,000 project for a start-up with financial difficulties and no payment received. Should the revenue be recognized?

Answer: No, do not recognize revenue.

Explanation:

  • Probability: Low probability of payment due to client’s financial issues.
  • Measurement: Amount is known, but uncertainty of collection affects reliability.

The probability criterion is not met, so revenue recognition is deferred.

3. Advance Payment for Services

A company receives a $1,000 advance payment for consulting services to be provided next month. Should a liability be recognized?

Answer: Yes, recognize $1,000 liability.

Explanation:

  • Probability: Probable obligation to provide services or refund.
  • Measurement: The amount ($1,000) is fixed and known.

Both criteria are met, so a liability (deferred revenue) is recognized.

4. Estimated Warranty Costs

A company sells products with a warranty, estimating $5,000 in future repair costs based on historical data. Should an expense be recognized?

Answer: Yes, recognize $5,000 expense.

Explanation:

  • Probability: Probable outflow based on historical warranty claims.
  • Measurement: The amount ($5,000) is reliably estimated from past data.

Both criteria are met, so a warranty expense and liability are recognized.

5. Contingent Legal Settlement

A company is sued, but the outcome is uncertain, and no reliable estimate of the settlement cost can be made. Should an expense be recognized?

Answer: No, do not recognize expense.

Explanation:

  • Probability: Outcome is uncertain, so outflow is not probable.
  • Measurement: No reliable estimate of the cost is available.

Neither criterion is met, so no expense is recognized, though disclosure may be required.

6. Subscription Revenue

A magazine publisher receives $120 for a one-year subscription, with magazines delivered monthly. Should revenue be recognized upfront?

Answer: No, recognize revenue over time.

Explanation:

  • Probability: Payment received, so inflow is probable.
  • Measurement: The amount ($120) is known, but services are provided over time.

Both criteria are met, but revenue is recognized as magazines are delivered (e.g., $10/month), with a liability for unearned revenue initially.

7. Employee Bonus

A company promises a $20,000 bonus to employees if sales targets are met, which is highly likely based on current performance. Should an expense be recognized?

Answer: Yes, recognize $20,000 expense.

Explanation:

  • Probability: High likelihood of meeting sales targets, so outflow is probable.
  • Measurement: The amount ($20,000) is fixed and known.

Both criteria are met, so the bonus expense and liability are recognized.

8. Uncertain Sales Commission

A salesperson earns a commission based on future sales, but the amount cannot be reliably estimated yet. Should an expense be recognized?

Answer: No, do not recognize expense.

Explanation:

  • Probability: Outflow is probable if sales occur, but not certain yet.
  • Measurement: The amount cannot be reliably estimated.

The measurement criterion is not met, so no expense is recognized until the amount is estimable.

9. Online Course Sale

An online platform sells a $200 course with immediate access to all materials, paid upfront via credit card. Should revenue be recognized?

Answer: Yes, recognize $200 revenue.

Explanation:

  • Probability: Payment received via credit card, so inflow is probable.
  • Measurement: The amount ($200) is fixed and known.

Both criteria are met, and the service (course access) is provided, so revenue is recognized immediately.
