Understanding the Profit & Loss Statement and Closing Entries

A guide to the temporary accounts on the P&L and how they are closed at the end of a period.

Commonly Used P&L Accounts

The Profit and Loss Statement (also known as the Income Statement) is a financial report that summarizes a company's revenues, costs, and expenses over a specific period. It is entirely composed of temporary accounts, which are closed out at the end of each period.

Revenue Accounts:

  • Sales Revenue: The income generated from the sale of goods or services.
  • Service Revenue: The income earned from providing services.
  • Interest Income: Revenue earned from investments or loans.

Expense Accounts:

  • Cost of Goods Sold (COGS): The direct costs attributable to the production of the goods sold by a company.
  • Salaries & Wages Expense: Costs for employee compensation.
  • Rent Expense: Costs for renting office space, equipment, etc.
  • Utilities Expense: Costs for electricity, water, and other utilities.
  • Advertising Expense: Costs for marketing and promotion.
  • Depreciation Expense: The non-cash expense for the wear and tear of long-term assets.

The Closing Process: Step-by-Step Journal Entries

The process of closing these accounts to the Profit or Loss Summary account and then to the Retained Earnings account is the formal procedure for determining and recording a company's net income (or loss).

Step 1: Close all Revenue Accounts

Revenue accounts typically have a credit balance. To close them and make their balance zero, you must debit each revenue account for its full balance. The total amount is then credited to the Profit or Loss Summary account.

Date Account Debit Credit
Dec 31 Sales Revenue $250,000
Dec 31 Service Revenue $80,000
Profit or Loss Summary $330,000
*To close all revenue accounts to the summary account.*

Step 2: Close all Expense Accounts

Expense accounts typically have a debit balance. To close them, you must credit each expense account for its full balance. The total amount is then debited to the Profit or Loss Summary account.

Date Account Debit Credit
Dec 31 Profit or Loss Summary $280,000
Cost of Goods Sold $150,000
Salaries & Wages Expense $80,000
Rent Expense $30,000
Advertising Expense $20,000
*To close all expense accounts to the summary account.*

Step 3: Close the Profit or Loss Summary Account

After the first two steps, the Profit or Loss Summary account's balance represents the net income or loss for the period ($330,000 - $280,000 = $50,000 Credit). This final balance is then transferred to the Retained Earnings account, which is a permanent equity account on the balance sheet.

Date Account Debit Credit
Dec 31 Profit or Loss Summary $50,000
Retained Earnings $50,000
*To close net income to Retained Earnings.*

By following this process, all temporary accounts are reset to zero, and the company's profitability for the period is officially recorded in its equity.
