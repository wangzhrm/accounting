Equity Components Summary

1. Share Capital (Contributed Equity)

Changes due to: transactions between the company and its owners (shareholders).

Increases:

  • Issue of new shares for cash or non-cash consideration.
    Scenario: A company issues 1,000 new shares at $10 per share for cash to raise capital for expansion.
    Dr. Cash $10,000
    Cr. Share Capital $10,000
  • Conversion of debt to equity.
    Scenario: A company converts $50,000 of outstanding bonds into 5,000 shares of common stock.
    Dr. Bonds Payable $50,000
    Cr. Share Capital $50,000

Decreases:

  • Share buybacks (repurchase of shares).
    Scenario: A company repurchases 500 of its own shares at $20 per share to reduce the number of outstanding shares.
    Dr. Share Capital $10,000
    Cr. Cash $10,000
  • Capital reduction or cancellation of shares.
    Scenario: A company cancels 2,000 shares as part of a capital reduction plan, returning $15,000 to shareholders.
    Dr. Share Capital $15,000
    Cr. Cash $15,000
👉 Key idea: Share capital changes only when the company issues or redeems shares — i.e., when owners directly contribute or withdraw funds.

2. Retained Earnings

Changes due to: the company’s profits and losses and dividend distributions.

Increases:

  • Net profit (income exceeds expenses).
    Scenario: A company reports a net profit of $100,000 for the fiscal year after all expenses are deducted from revenues.
    Dr. Income Summary $100,000
    Cr. Retained Earnings $100,000

Decreases:

  • Net loss.
    Scenario: A company incurs a net loss of $40,000 due to higher expenses than revenues in a challenging market.
    Dr. Retained Earnings $40,000
    Cr. Income Summary $40,000
  • Payment of dividends to shareholders.
    Scenario: A company declares and pays $30,000 in cash dividends to its shareholders from accumulated profits.
    Dr. Retained Earnings $30,000
    Cr. Cash $30,000
  • Prior-period adjustments (e.g., correction of past errors).
    Scenario: A company discovers an error from the previous year that overstated revenue by $20,000, requiring a correction.
    Dr. Retained Earnings $20,000
    Cr. Revenue $20,000
👉 Key idea: Retained earnings reflect internal performance — how much of the company’s accumulated profit is kept rather than distributed.

3. Reserves

Changes due to: certain revaluations, capital adjustments, or appropriations of profit.

Examples of reserves and how they arise:

  • Revaluation Reserve: from upward revaluation of assets (e.g., PPE).
    Scenario: A company revalues its property, plant, and equipment upward by $50,000 based on a professional appraisal.
    Dr. PPE $50,000
    Cr. Revaluation Reserve $50,000
  • Foreign Currency Translation Reserve: from translating foreign subsidiaries’ financials.
    Scenario: A company translates the financial statements of its foreign subsidiary, resulting in a $15,000 gain due to exchange rate changes.
    Dr. Other Comprehensive Income $15,000
    Cr. Foreign Currency Translation Reserve $15,000
  • General Reserve: portion of retained earnings set aside by management.
    Scenario: Management decides to transfer $25,000 from retained earnings to a general reserve for future contingencies.
    Dr. Retained Earnings $25,000
    Cr. General Reserve $25,000
  • Capital Reserve: from non-trading gains (e.g., bargain purchase in acquisition).
    Scenario: A company acquires another entity at a bargain price, resulting in a $40,000 gain recognized as a capital reserve.
    Dr. Assets (Net) $40,000
    Cr. Capital Reserve $40,000
👉 Key idea: Reserves are not from share transactions or regular profits but from specific accounting adjustments or internal allocations.
