Accounting Journal Entry Practice

Test your knowledge of basic journal entries and key accounting principles.

1. September 1, 2025: Jane starts a consulting business, "Jane's Consulting," and invests $10,000 of her personal funds into a business bank account.

Hint: Accounts involved are Cash and Owner's Capital.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Sep 1, 2025 Cash 10,000
Owner's Capital 10,000
To record initial investment by the owner

Explanation:

The business's Cash (an asset) increases, which is a debit. The owner's equity (Owner's Capital) also increases, which is a credit.

2. September 2, 2025: Jane's Consulting pays $1,500 for a month's rent for its office space.

Hint: Accounts involved are Rent Expense and Cash.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Sep 2, 2025 Rent Expense 1,500
Cash 1,500
To record the payment of monthly rent

Explanation:

Rent Expense (an expense) increases, which is a debit. Cash (an asset) decreases, which is a credit.

3. September 5, 2025: Jane's Consulting purchases office supplies on credit for $300.

Hint: Accounts involved are Office Supplies and Accounts Payable.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Sep 5, 2025 Office Supplies 300
Accounts Payable 300
To record the purchase of supplies on account

Explanation:

Office Supplies (an asset) increases, which is a debit. Accounts Payable (a liability) also increases, which is a credit.

4. September 8, 2025: Jane's Consulting provides consulting services and immediately receives $2,000 in cash.

Hint: Accounts involved are Cash and Service Revenue.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Sep 8, 2025 Cash 2,000
Service Revenue 2,000
To record cash received for services rendered

Explanation:

Cash (an asset) increases, which is a debit. Service Revenue (an income) also increases, which is a credit.

5. September 10, 2025: Jane's Consulting provides consulting services on credit for $3,500. The customer will pay later.

Hint: Accounts involved are Accounts Receivable and Service Revenue.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Sep 10, 2025 Accounts Receivable 3,500
Service Revenue 3,500
To record services provided on credit

Explanation:

Accounts Receivable (an asset, the right to collect cash) increases, which is a debit. Service Revenue (an income) also increases, which is a credit.

6. September 12, 2025: Jane's Consulting pays its employee's wages of $800.

Answer:

Cash 800
To record payment of wages

Explanation:

Wages Expense (an expense) increases, which is a debit. Cash (an asset) decreases, which is a credit.

7. September 15, 2025: The customer from September 10 pays $3,500 in cash for the services received.

Hint: Accounts involved are Cash and Accounts Receivable.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Sep 15, 2025 Cash 3,500
Accounts Receivable 3,500
To record cash received from a customer on account

Explanation:

Cash (an asset) increases, which is a debit. Accounts Receivable (an asset) decreases, which is a credit. The revenue was already recorded on September 10.

8. September 18, 2025: Jane's Consulting pays the $300 owed for the office supplies purchased on September 5.

Sep 18, 2025 Accounts Payable 300
Cash 300
To record the payment for supplies previously purchased on account

Explanation:

Accounts Payable (a liability) decreases, which is a debit. Cash (an asset) decreases, which is a credit.

11. September 28, 2025: Jane's Consulting receives a utility bill for $150. Payment is due next month.

Explanation:

Utilities Expense (an expense) increases, which is a debit. Accounts Payable (a liability) also increases, which is a credit.

12. September 30, 2025: Jane's Consulting pays $1,200 in advance for six months of software licenses. The licenses are effective from October 1, 2025.

Hint: Accounts involved are Prepaid Expenses and Cash.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Sep 30, 2025 Prepaid Software Licenses 1,200
Cash 1,200
To record cash paid for future software expenses

Explanation:

Prepaid Software Licenses (an asset) increases, which is a debit. Cash (an asset) decreases, which is a credit. The expense will be recognized later as the benefit is consumed.

13. October 1, 2025: A customer gives Jane's Consulting a signed cheque for $500 for services to be provided in December.

Hint: Consider the Revenue Recognition Principle and the Accrual Basis of Accounting. No journal entry is needed until the service is performed or cash is received.

Answer:

No journal entry is recorded.

Explanation:

Under the accrual basis of accounting, revenue is recognized when it is earned, not when a purchase order is received. The purchase order is just an agreement; no service has been performed yet.

14. October 5, 2025: Jane's Consulting signs a contract to rent a new office space for one year, effective November 1, 2025, for $2,000 per month.

Hint: This is a future event. No economic transaction has occurred yet. Consider the Accrual Basis of Accounting.

Answer:

No journal entry is recorded.

Explanation:

Signing a contract does not constitute a transaction. The journal entry will be made when the business pays the rent or incurs the expense in November.

15. October 8, 2025: Jane's Consulting buys a computer for $1,200 for the owner’s son to use for his homework. The funds were withdrawn from the business's bank account.

Hint: Consider the Entity Principle.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Oct 8, 2025 Owner's Drawings 1,200
Cash 1,200
To record owner's withdrawal for personal use (purchase of computer for son)

Explanation:

The business and the owner are separate entities (Entity Principle). The computer is for personal use, so it is treated as a withdrawal by the owner, not a business asset.

16. October 12, 2025: Jane's Consulting receives a bill from the power company for $200. The bill is in the owner's personal name, but the power was used to run the office. Jane plans to pay it personally.

Hint: Consider the Entity Principle. Even though the electricity was used for the business, the transaction is not between the business and the power company.

Answer:

No journal entry is recorded.

Explanation:

The business and the owner are separate entities. Since the bill is in the owner’s name and will be paid personally, the business has no liability or transaction to record.

17. October 15, 2025: Jane's Consulting sells consulting services to a customer for 3,000 Euros. The exchange rate is 1 Euro = $1.10.

Hint: Consider the Monetary Unit Principle. The transaction must be recorded in the business's home currency.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Oct 15, 2025 Accounts Receivable 3,300
Service Revenue 3,300
To record services provided to a foreign customer (3,000 Euros x $1.10)

Explanation:

The Monetary Unit Principle states that all business transactions must be recorded in the home currency. The 3,000 Euros must be converted to US dollars ($3,000 x 1.10 = $3,300).

18. October 18, 2025: Jane's Consulting's main competitor unexpectedly closes down its business, which is likely to result in a significant increase in Jane's business.

Hint: This is an event, but it's not a transaction. Consider the Accrual Basis of Accounting.

Answer:

No journal entry is recorded.

Explanation:

This is a future event with no immediate economic impact on the business's financial position that can be reliably measured. No journal entry is recorded until Jane provides services and earns revenue.

19. October 20, 2025: Jane's Consulting receives a payment of 2,500 Canadian Dollars from a customer. The exchange rate is 1 CAD = $0.75.

Hint: Consider the Monetary Unit Principle.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Oct 20, 2025 Cash 1,875
Accounts Receivable / Service Revenue 1,875
To record cash received from a foreign customer (2,500 CAD x $0.75)

Explanation:

The Monetary Unit Principle requires transactions to be recorded in the home currency. The 2,500 Canadian Dollars must be converted to US dollars ($2,500 x 0.75 = $1,875).

20. October 25, 2025: Jane's Consulting purchases a new laptop for $1,500 and pays with her personal credit card.

Hint: Consider the Entity Principle and what this transaction means for the business's records.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Oct 25, 2025 Equipment (or Office Equipment) 1,500
Owner's Capital 1,500
To record the purchase of a laptop with the owner's personal funds

Explanation:

The laptop is a business asset, so the business records the asset (Equipment). The payment was made with personal funds, which is a direct investment by the owner, increasing their capital.

21. October 28, 2025: Jane's Consulting is sued by a former client for a minor breach of contract. A lawyer advises that the suit is frivolous and will likely be dismissed.

Hint: Consider the Monetary Unit Principle. Can the financial impact be reliably measured?

Answer:

No journal entry is recorded.

Explanation:

Since the financial outcome is not yet known and cannot be reliably measured, no journal entry is recorded. This event may be disclosed in the financial statement notes if it is considered significant.

22. November 1, 2025: A customer gives Jane's Consulting a signed cheque for $500 for services to be provided in December.

Hint: Consider the Accrual Basis of Accounting. Has the revenue been earned yet?

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Nov 1, 2025 Cash 500
Unearned Service Revenue 500
To record cash received for services to be provided in the future

Explanation:

The revenue has not been earned yet. Therefore, the cash received is recorded as a liability, Unearned Service Revenue, until the services are performed in December.

23. December 31, 2025: At the end of the year, Jane's Consulting counts its office supplies and finds that $150 worth of supplies are left. The beginning balance was $0, and a $300 purchase was made on Sep 5, 2025.

Hint: This is an adjusting entry to account for supplies used. Consider the Accrual Basis of Accounting.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Dec 31, 2025 Supplies Expense 150
Office Supplies 150
To record office supplies used during the period ($300 - $150)

Explanation:

An adjusting entry is needed to recognize the expense of the supplies that were used during the period. The amount used is the initial purchase ($300) minus the supplies on hand ($150), which equals $150.

24. December 31, 2025: A customer who received services on December 15 for $800 has not yet paid. The invoice was sent on December 16, and the payment is due on January 15, 2026.

Hint: Consider the Accrual Basis of Accounting and the Accounting Period Convention.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Dec 31, 2025 Accounts Receivable 800
Service Revenue 800
To accrue revenue earned but not yet collected

Explanation:

Even though the cash hasn't been received, the revenue was earned in December. The Accrual Basis of Accounting and the Accounting Period Convention require that revenue be recognized in the period it is earned.

25. December 31, 2025: An employee worked for the last week of December, earning $400. The payday is January 5, 2026.

Hint: Consider the Accrual Basis of Accounting and the Accounting Period Convention.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Dec 31, 2025 Wages Expense 400
Wages Payable 400
To accrue wages incurred but not yet paid

Explanation:

The expense was incurred in December, even though the cash will be paid in January. The Accrual Basis of Accounting requires that the expense be recognized in the period it was incurred.

26. December 31, 2025: The $1,200 in prepaid software licenses purchased on September 30, 2025, have been used for three months (October, November, and December).

Hint: This is an adjusting entry to recognize the expense of the asset used. Consider the Accrual Basis of Accounting.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Dec 31, 2025 Software License Expense 600
Prepaid Software Licenses 600
To record the portion of prepaid licenses used ($1,200 / 6 months x 3 months)

Explanation:

The prepaid asset has been consumed over three months. The expense for this period needs to be recognized. The monthly expense is $1,200 / 6 = $200. For three months, the expense is $200 x 3 = $600.

27. December 31, 2025: Jane's Consulting is informed that a major economic recession is expected in the next six months.

Hint: This is a future event. Can it be reliably measured? Consider the Going Concern Assumption.

Answer:

No journal entry is recorded.

Explanation:

The Going Concern Assumption assumes that a business will continue to operate for the foreseeable future. A future economic recession is an event that does not constitute a transaction and cannot be reliably measured. The business will continue to operate and record transactions as they occur.

28. January 15, 2026: Jane's Consulting pays the $800 from the December 31 transaction (Question 24) that was previously billed to the customer.

Hint: The revenue was already recorded. This is a payment on an outstanding account. This transaction relates to the Accrual Basis of Accounting.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Jan 15, 2026 Cash 800
Accounts Receivable 800
To record cash received from a customer on account

Explanation:

This is a simple cash collection. The revenue was already recorded in December, so this entry simply moves the balance from Accounts Receivable to Cash.

29. January 5, 2026: Jane's Consulting pays the employee's wages of $400 for the last week of December (Question 25).

Hint: This is a payment for a liability that was already recorded. This transaction relates to the Accrual Basis of Accounting.

Answer:

Date Account Debit ($) Credit ($)
Jan 5, 2026 Wages Payable 400
Cash 400
To record payment of wages previously accrued

Explanation:

The expense was already recorded in December. This entry is a simple payment that reduces the liability, Wages Payable, and decreases cash.

30. February 1, 2026: The business owner, Jane, purchases a new personal car for $30,000. She uses a personal bank account.

Hint: Consider the Entity Principle.

Answer:

No journal entry is recorded.

Explanation:

The business and the owner are separate entities (Entity Principle). Since the purchase was made with personal funds and for personal use, it has no impact on the business's financial records.
