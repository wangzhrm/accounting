The Separate Entity Principle 🏢

Accountants must follow the principle that a business is a separate entity from its owner(s). Let's see if these scenarios **observe** or **violate** this key rule.

Scenario 1: Sarah, the owner of a small graphic design firm, uses the business's debit card to pay for her daughter's university tuition fee.

Does this transaction observe or violate the separate entity principle?

❌ Violates. The tuition fee is a personal expense of the owner. Using the business's funds for a personal transaction **violates** the principle by mixing personal and business finances.

Scenario 2: John, a sole proprietor, uses his personal credit card to buy a new computer for his home. He does not record this purchase in his business's accounting system.

Does this transaction observe or violate the separate entity principle?

✅ Observes. John correctly kept his personal transaction separate from his business's records. By not recording it, he is adhering to the principle that his business is a distinct entity.

Scenario 3: The owner of "SmartTech Solutions" buys a car for the business. She records the purchase in the business's books but titles the car in her personal name.

Does this transaction observe or violate the separate entity principle?

❌ Violates. While the owner intended the car for the business, titling it in her personal name blurs the line between business assets and personal assets, thereby **violating** the separate entity principle. The car's legal ownership should align with its intended use and accounting recognition.

Scenario 4: The business, "Global Imports Inc.," sells a product on credit to the owner's friend. The business records an account receivable and expects to be paid in 30 days.

Does this transaction observe or violate the separate entity principle?

✅ Observes. The business correctly recorded the sale and the resulting financial obligation (the account receivable). The fact that the customer is the owner's friend is irrelevant; the transaction is a legitimate business event and is recorded as such.
