Asset Recognition Criteria Practice Quiz (10 Questions) 🧠

Please circle the correct answer for each question. Provide brief explanations where required.

1. Which of the following is a key criterion for recognizing an item as an asset in accounting?

A. The item must be sold within the next fiscal year.

B. The item must have the potential to generate future economic benefits.

C. The item must be fully depreciated.

D. The item must be used only by the organization’s employees.

2. A company purchases a vehicle that is in poor condition and would cost more to repair than to replace. Why should this vehicle not be recognized as an asset?

A. It lacks control by the organization.

B. It cannot generate net future economic benefits.

C. Its value cannot be reliably measured.

D. Its existence is uncertain.

3. What does it mean for an organization to have "control" over an asset?

A. The organization must own the asset legally.

B. The organization can direct the use of the asset and obtain its benefits.

C. The organization must insure the asset against loss.

D. The organization must have exclusive access to the asset’s location.

4. A retail company uses a public parking lot for its customers. Why can’t the parking lot be recognized as an asset on the company’s balance sheet?

A. The parking lot has no future economic benefits.

B. The company does not control the parking lot.

C. The parking lot’s value is too uncertain to measure.

D. The parking lot is a contingent asset.

5. Why is the existence of an asset important for recognition in accounting?

A. The asset must be physically present in the organization’s premises.

B. The asset’s existence must be certain or highly probable.

C. The asset must be recorded in the general ledger.

D. The asset must have a historical cost.

6. A company expects to receive a patent approval for a new invention, but it is still under review. Why should this patent not be recognized as an asset yet?

A. The patent’s value cannot be reliably measured.

B. The company does not control the patent.

C. The patent’s existence is uncertain until approved.

D. The patent will not generate economic benefits.

7. A tech company invests heavily in developing a new software product, but market success is uncertain. Why are these development costs typically expensed rather than recognized as an asset?

A. The software lacks control by the company.

B. The probability of future economic benefits is low.

C. The software’s value is too high to measure.

D. The software is a contingent asset.

8. Why is reliable measurement important for asset recognition?

A. The asset must have a monetary value that can be accurately determined.

B. The asset must be appraised annually.

C. The asset’s cost must exceed a certain threshold.

D. The asset must be compared to market standards.

9. A company’s internally developed customer loyalty program is valuable but has no external transaction to base its value on. Why is it not recognized as an asset?

A. It lacks future economic benefits.

B. The company does not control the program.

C. Its value cannot be reliably measured.

D. Its existence is uncertain.

10. A manufacturing company spends $50,000 on a feasibility study for a new factory, but the project is deemed unviable. Why should these costs not be recognized as an asset?

A. The costs lack control by the company.

B. The costs do not lead to future economic benefits.

C. The costs are too uncertain to measure.

D. The costs are a contingent asset.
