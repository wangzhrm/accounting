ACCOUNTING SCANDAL

WorldCom Inflates Revenue Through Premature and Fictitious Sales

By Financial Investigations Desk
Updated 2 hours ago

WorldCom, once a leading U.S. telecommunications company, artificially inflated its revenue in the late 1990s and early 2000s through aggressive accounting practices. The company brought forward sales revenue, recognizing income from services that had not yet been delivered, in an effort to meet shareholder expectations and secure executive bonuses.

Former WorldCom CEO Bernard Ebbers leaves court after hearing (2004)

In addition, WorldCom faked sales revenue by creating transactions with its own subsidiaries. These intercompany transactions, which often involved services that never actually occurred, were recorded as legitimate revenue on the books. While these transactions appeared to increase the company's earnings and asset base, they did not reflect any real economic activity.

"The WorldCom scandal represents one of the most egregious accounting frauds in history, fundamentally breaching the trust of investors and shaking confidence in corporate America." - SEC Chairman

These practices allowed WorldCom to report growth that far exceeded its actual operational performance, misleading investors and analysts. The fraud ultimately contributed to one of the largest accounting scandals in U.S. history, leading to billions in overstated revenue and the company's eventual bankruptcy.

Key Facts

  • $11 billion in fraudulent accounting
  • Fraud occurred from 1999 to 2002
  • 5 executives convicted
  • 30,000 jobs lost after bankruptcy
  • Stock fell from $64 to $0.20 per share

Aftermath & Consequences

WorldCom filed for bankruptcy in July 2002
CEO Bernard Ebbers sentenced to 25 years in prison
Sarbanes-Oxley Act passed to improve corporate governance
Investors lost an estimated $180 billion
